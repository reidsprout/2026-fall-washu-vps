Media Response 07 - "The Coming Age of Calm Technology"

It's always interesting to see people's theories and predictions on how the web will work in 20 years, because we can compare them to where we're actually at. Before reading, I had never heard anything about the concept of "calm technology," an already troubling sign that we are not at the point the article predicts quite yet. I still — and probably many others — would not associate the web or information technoloy with the sate of being calm. The internet is kind of a violent place. We are constantly being bombarded with advertisement, news, opinions, slop; apps and websites are specifically designed to stay in our center of attention for as long as possible; everyone is literally, chemically, addicted to their phones. As I read, I found myself WISHING that tech like my phone could exist purely in my periphery, and that it had less control in my life. While it's true that many are calmed by technoloy, like watching a youtube video or scrolling, it's almost more like being drugged. There's less ease in changing states than what the article discusses.

So is calm technology possible? Yes, I think so. And I think we have probably achieved it in some places, but because the whole point of calm technology is to be unnoticably, seamlessly integrated into your life, these don't jump out at you. I think of things like smart watches and home assistants (like Alexa?) could fit in to the idea of calm technology. But compared to the article's prediction that "calm technology will play a central role in a more humanly empowered twenty-first century," we seem behind schedule. Maybe we keep getting distracted by shiny new toys (AI), or maybe "calm technology" really is an oxymoron.
