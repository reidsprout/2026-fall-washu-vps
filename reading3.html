Reading Response 03 - "My Website is a Shifting House..."

1. What did you think about the essay? Did anything in particular resonate?

I think a lot of people (myself included), when we think of the internet, we think of what a stressful mess it is - the constant surveillance, advertisement, and barrages of bad opinions. I like that this article tried to ask how can we make it better? How can we recenter the individual? How can we move the definition of a website - and the internet - away from what big corporations have made it into?

2. Laurel proposes a website could be a river, a garden a thrown rock. If you have a website, what could your website be?

I really like the thrown rock metaphor in particular; the idea of tossing a piece of you into a big void is very enticing, because it relieves the pressure of perfectionism. Maybe, something of a similar vein - a website as a message in a bottle. You never know who it might reach! It might even make it's way back to you when you least expect it.

3. What can you with a website that you make yourself that you can’t do if someone else makes your website?

I think the main advantage to making your own website is the amount of control you get, and the self-empowerment that comes with that. There's no template that will exactly fit an individual's needs, so by making your own, you can construct a shape that will exactly fit you, and change as you grow.
