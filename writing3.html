Writing exercise 03 - When do you cherish the slow or hard way of doing something? Why?

Something I recently discovered is letterpress printing, and it immediately came to mind when thinking about this prompt. For actual printing, it's completely inefficient, archaeic, and makes no sense. However, when I interned at a letterpress print studio this summer, I totally fell in love with the slower process and designing stuff with it. First, you have to sort through cases and cases of type to find one that's the right size and font for what you're thinking of (I love how, unlike a computer, this is limited. You have to work with what you have.). Then, you have to typeset each line letter by letter (and spaces, and punctuation) backwards, making sure to not misspell anything. When getting type ready to print, you have to build a lockup out of furniture and metal spacing to make it exactly rectangular, so each side gets even amounts of pressure applied to it. This whole process can take hours, or days, for a single page of text, a greeting card, or whatever you want to print. But there's something so human about it. Unlike designing with a computer, you are working with your hands, combing through actual drawers of type instead of a dropdown menu. You spend time thinking about the ink and the paper, and what it will feel like. On older type, there will be artifacts - scuffs and chips. Sometimes, typecases will be incomplete, and you'll have to sub in a 0 for an O to make it work. It's a nice break from my usual coursework, and offers challenges that a computer project can't give you.
